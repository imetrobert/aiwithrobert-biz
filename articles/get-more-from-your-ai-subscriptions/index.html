Getting more from the AI subscriptions you already pay for

Many small businesses pay for an AI assistant and use it for a few occasional questions. Before buying another tool, it is worth checking how much of the one you have is actually working for you.

1. List what you pay for

Write down every AI subscription across the team, who uses it and how often. Overlap is common, and so are licences that nobody opens.

2. Name the repetitive tasks

Look for tasks that recur every week: answering similar emails, preparing quotes, summarizing documents, writing reports. These are the best candidates.

3. Turn good prompts into shared ones

When someone gets a good result, write the prompt down in a shared place, with an example of a good input and output. Reusable prompts are the cheapest improvement available.

4. Check what your plan can do

Features differ by plan, connectors, API access and third-party services. Confirm what your subscription includes, and note anything that would need an extra service.

5. Add human review where it matters

Keep a person in the loop for anything that goes to a client or involves a decision. AI drafts quickly; people stay accountable.

When to ask for help

If this feels like a lot, a scoped working session can do it with you: review your subscriptions, document tailored prompts and configure workflows around recurring tasks, with a walkthrough. Beginners with no subscription yet are welcome too.
